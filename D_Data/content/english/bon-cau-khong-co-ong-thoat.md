Imagine a toilet with no drain. Every flush sends the waste through a hidden
pipe and back into the water tank. The next flush looks familiar. After a
while, the owner says, “See? Everything that enters this toilet agrees with
what was already here.”

A closed mind can be built the same way.

It reads only people who copy one another. It treats every challenge as proof
of an enemy. It produces a claim, hears the claim repeated, then calls the
echo outside support.

The number of voices may grow while the number of sources stays at one.

The owner may even invite visitors. But anyone who says the water smells wrong
is called dirty and removed. The room is technically open. Its rule for entry
keeps it closed.

An outside pipe must be able to carry in something the tank did not make. More
important, the plumbing must allow that new water to change what comes next.
Merely displaying an objection before grinding it into the old answer is not
contact.

Try feeding the system a result it did not expect. Watch whether it changes a
rule, lowers its trust, or asks for a better check. If it only invents a new
reason why the failure proves success, the pipe bends back into the tank.

To inspect a system, follow the pipes. Does new material enter from outside?
Can a result travel back and change the starting rule? Are several reports
independent, or do they drink from the same tank? What happens to a fact the
system did not predict?

A belief can survive many rounds because it is strong. It can also survive
because nothing is allowed to touch it.

Do not count flushes. Find the drain.
