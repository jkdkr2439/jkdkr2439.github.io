This piece is not addressed to the old generation of philosophy in Vietnam. That generation may have been trapped in its time: trapped by the conditions of translation, by the education system, by the lack of materials. I have no great desire to discuss them here.

I am speaking to the younger generation studying philosophy. Those under thirty-five. Those who have the internet, Google, digital libraries, papers, dictionaries, A.I., video lectures, forums, translation tools - everything the generations before them did not have.

And I will say it plainly: if you are under thirty-five, have every instrument of this age within reach, study epistemology, and still cannot ask one very simple question - *what is that research question doing?* - then you are genuinely useless.

Not useless because you lack the terminology. You know far too much terminology. Not useless because you do not read. You read quite a lot. You are useless because you do not think. Your thought is led about on a leash, yet you mistake it for independence because you can repeat a few grand questions in a voice of counterfeit humility.

In epistemology, you often encounter the question: “What is knowledge?” It sounds familiar, does it not? So familiar that it seems perfectly ordinary. But have you ever asked yourselves what that sentence - “What is knowledge?” - is for?

Not what it asks on the surface. The surface is obvious. But at a deeper level, what is it doing? What operation does it open? Down what path is it forcing thought to travel?

Read to the bottom, and every question in epistemology is performing the same act: **the pursuit of a definition.** A definition of knowledge. Of belief. Of justification. Of truth. Of knowing.

Epistemology does not begin with knowledge. It begins with the question of the defining conditions under which something may be called knowledge. Before asking what we know, we must ask what is allowed to count as knowing.

Thinking does not begin when you possess a great many answers. It begins when you know to ask: **what is this question trying to define?**
