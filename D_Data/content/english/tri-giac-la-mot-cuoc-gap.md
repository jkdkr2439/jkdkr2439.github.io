A cup sits on a table. From the doorway its handle is invisible. From the
window the glazed surface throws back a white glare. In the hand it is cool,
heavy, and slightly rough beneath the thumb. None of these encounters invents
the cup. None exhausts it.

Perception is often described under the pressure of a false choice. Either the
world simply prints itself upon a passive mind, or the mind manufactures a
private world from within. The first picture cannot explain why position,
capacity, expectation, and action matter. The second cannot explain why the
world resists us, surprises us, and corrects us.

The better starting point is an encounter:

> Perception is an achievement of a situated organism in contact with a world
> that constrains what can successfully be perceived.

This is not yet a complete theory of perception. It is a rule against two
premature reductions.

## The world contributes resistance

If perception were free invention, error would be difficult to distinguish
from success. Yet the world does not cooperate equally with every perceptual
judgment. A painted door does not open. A shadow approached from another angle
loses the shape that first resembled a person. The cup believed empty spills
water when tilted.

Perceptual success therefore involves more than coherence inside a subject.
It involves continued contact, action, and correction. What we perceive must
survive some of the consequences of being true.

## The perceiver contributes capacities

Contact is never capacity-free. Eyes respond to a limited range of light.
Bodies occupy positions. Movement changes what becomes visible. Hunger,
fatigue, training, fear, and expectation can alter what is noticed and how an
ambiguous scene is organized.

To say that the perceiver contributes is not to say that contribution equals
fabrication. A key contributes to the opening of a lock; it does not invent the
lock. A measuring instrument contributes a scale and mode of display; it does
not therefore create the measured process.

The useful question is not whether the subject contributes, but what is
contributed and how that contribution may be checked.

## Perception has an action horizon

Objects do not appear merely as bundles of neutral properties. A stair is
climbable, a chair sittable, a path passable, a ledge dangerous. These
possibilities depend jointly on worldly structure and bodily capacity. The
same opening may be a passage for a child and an obstacle for an adult. The
opening has not changed, but the relation has.

This relational character explains why perspective is not always a defect.
Perspective can disclose facts available only from a certain mode of
engagement. A mechanic hears a failing bearing where another person hears only
engine noise. Training has not freed the mechanic from the world. It has made
the mechanic answerable to finer differences within it.

## Illusion teaches dependence, not universal deception

Illusions matter because perception normally works. A bent appearance of a
stick in water reveals that optical access depends on conditions. It does not
prove that sticks, water, and correction are inaccessible. Indeed, calling the
appearance misleading requires a more stable relation among viewpoints,
touch, measurement, and prediction.

Error is often detected through conflict:

- one angle disagrees with another;
- sight disagrees with touch;
- expectation disagrees with action;
- one observer disagrees with others;
- an immediate judgment disagrees with a controlled instrument.

The conflict does not automatically tell us which channel is right. It gives
inquiry somewhere to begin.

## Three mistakes about perspective

The first mistake is to treat a perspective as the whole. What appears from
here is silently promoted into what exists without qualification.

The second is to treat every perspective as sovereign. Because access is
situated, criticism is declared impossible. But perspectives overlap, make
predictions, guide action, and encounter resistance. They are therefore
comparable even when they are not identical.

The third is to believe that adding perspectives mechanically produces
completeness. Ten witnesses may share one blind spot. Multiple measurements
may depend on the same faulty calibration. Diversity of access helps only when
the routes are relevant and at least partly independent.

## The fantasy of the innocent eye

People sometimes praise an “unbiased eye” as though excellence in perception
required the removal of every expectation. An eye without expectation would
not be innocent. It would be useless. To recognize a footprint, read a scan,
or hear a false note is to bring learned discrimination to the encounter.

The serious distinction is not between expectation and its absence. It is
between expectations that remain answerable to correction and expectations
that rearrange every correction into agreement. The tracker uses a model to
question the ground. The fanatic uses the ground to flatter the model.

This is why perceptual discipline often requires deliberate inconvenience:
change the angle, slow the exposure, ask another observer, touch what vision
has settled too quickly, or place the favored interpretation beside a rival.
The world is under no obligation to correct us in the channel we find most
dignified.

## Perception is corrigible through movement

The static image has dominated theories of seeing because it is easy to draw.
Actual perceivers turn their heads, approach, retreat, handle, compare, and
wait. Movement is not an afterthought added to perception. It is one of the
ways appearance is interrogated.

A surface that looks smooth at a distance acquires grain in the hand. A shape
that seemed circular becomes elliptical when the observer moves. Each change
reveals a lawful dependency between appearance and route. What first looked
like instability becomes a method of learning structure.

## A perception audit

When a perceptual claim matters, ask:

1. What worldly feature is supposed to constrain the judgment?
2. Which capacities and position made that feature available?
3. What action or changed viewpoint could test the appearance?
4. Which conditions are known to degrade this channel?
5. Is disagreement caused by different access, different concepts, or a
   different state of the world?
6. Are the checking routes genuinely independent?

Perception is neither a window without glass nor a theatre sealed from the
world. It is a disciplined vulnerability: the world can reach us, but only
through forms of life capable of being reached.

Yet even within one perceptual field, not everything is received equally.
Some things advance; others recede. That selection is the work of attention.
