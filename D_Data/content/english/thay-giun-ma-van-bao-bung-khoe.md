You say your stomach is healthy.

Someone asks, “What would show you it is not?”

You answer, “Nothing. Pain proves it is cleaning itself. Blood proves the bad
things are leaving. Worms prove the cure has reached deep.”

Your claim has locked every door.

A living belief must allow the world to answer back. This does not mean one
odd sign should kill it. Tests can fail. Noise happens. Strong beliefs may
need strong counter-signs. But there must be some possible result that lowers
your trust or changes the claim.

Otherwise you are not protecting knowledge. You are protecting a sentence
from having to meet anything outside itself.

Change does not always mean throwing the whole belief away. You may keep the
main claim and narrow its range. You may lower your trust. You may replace one
cause while keeping the event. You may admit that the rule works for adults
but failed for this child.

That is how repair usually looks. A cracked chair may need one leg replaced,
not a fire. But if every crack is painted over and called decoration, the chair
has no repair rule at all.

Set the condition before the unwanted result arrives when possible. It is easy
to move the gate after seeing what came through it. A test written after the
answer often becomes a servant of the answer.

Before defending a belief, name the condition for repair. What would count
against it? How much would be enough? Which part would change first? If every
possible result becomes new praise for the same answer, the test is theatre.

An open door does not make a house weak. It lets you leave when the house is
on fire.
