You know your birth date, the existence of cities you have never visited, the
contents of medicines, the age of the Earth, and the route of tomorrow's train
through testimony. Even when you “look it up yourself,” you usually inspect a
record produced by others.

The fantasy of the independent knower survives only because dependence has
become invisible.

> Testimony is not a second-rate substitute for knowledge. It is a basic route
> by which finite knowers extend one another's access.

## Testimony is a transmission chain

A testimonial claim travels through roles:

```text
event or inquiry -> observer -> record -> transmitter -> hearer -> use
```

One person may occupy several roles. Each transition has different failure
conditions. The observer may be mistaken. The record may omit context. The
transmitter may distort. The hearer may misunderstand. A true sentence may be
used outside the domain in which it was warranted.

“Who said it?” is therefore only the beginning.

## Trust is neither blindness nor personal verification

If every hearer had to reproduce every source's inquiry, testimony would add
almost no knowledge. Responsible trust must often precede personal
verification.

But trust can be structured. We can examine proximity to the event, relevant
competence, incentives, track record, correction mechanisms, corroboration,
and signs of distortion. The goal is not to prove that a speaker cannot err.
It is to estimate the reliability of this transmission for this claim.

## Sincerity is not accuracy

A speaker may be honest and wrong. Another may be unreliable in general yet
correct on one occasion. Moral character matters to testimony, but it does not
replace access and competence.

This distinction prevents two common shortcuts. Affection is treated as proof:
“I know this person; therefore the claim is true.” Hostility is treated as
refutation: “That group lies; therefore this report is false.” Both replace
claim-level assessment with identity.

## Corroboration requires independence

Agreement strengthens testimony when reports arise through sufficiently
independent routes. A thousand reposts may descend from one anonymous line.
Several newspapers may reproduce one agency report. Apparent plurality can be
an echo.

Trace the lineage. If two reports share a source, they may still add value by
preserving or checking the transmission, but they should not be counted as two
independent observations.

## Defeaters can be local

Discovering that a witness had poor visibility weakens visual detail. It need
not defeat everything the witness reports. Discovering an institutional
conflict of interest raises scrutiny. It does not make every output false.

Good criticism targets the link it has reason to distrust. Total rejection is
often intellectually cheaper than diagnosis.

## Testimony is often communal before it is personal

The neat image of one speaker telling one hearer obscures much of modern
knowledge. A report may be produced by a laboratory team, edited by an
institution, summarized by a journalist, ranked by a search system, and quoted
by a friend. Asking whether the final speaker is honest does not reach the
machinery that made the sentence available.

Collective production creates strengths no individual possesses: divided
labor, checking, preservation, and specialized instruments. It also creates
diffuse responsibility. When a claim fails, each participant can point to the
next link. The sentence had many parents and suddenly no guardian.

Responsible testimony therefore requires provenance that survives
compression. The hearer need not receive the entire archive, but should be
able to recover what kind of source produced the claim, how directly it
encountered the subject, and where uncertainty was removed for convenience.

## Distrust can be credulous

The person who rejects an official account often imagines himself independent.
He may simply have transferred trust to a source that flatters his independence.
Distrust of one channel does not dissolve dependence; it chooses another
channel, frequently with fewer records and weaker correction.

There is no shame in reliance. The shame begins when reliance dresses as
sovereignty and refuses to name its supplier.

## A testimony audit

Ask:

1. Who had the nearest relevant access?
2. How was the claim recorded and transmitted?
3. Which links are competent in the required domain?
4. What incentives or pressures act on each link?
5. Are corroborating routes independent?
6. Which part of the claim is weakened by a known defect?
7. Can the claim be checked through another kind of access?

Testimony distributes knowledge because no individual can master every domain.
That distribution creates a special role, and a special danger: expertise.
