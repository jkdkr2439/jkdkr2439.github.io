<div id="part-1">I. Protection is real</div>

<section id="ch-1"><h2><small>01 / 13</small>No intrusion</h2><p>The first life I saved was a sheet of corrugated iron.</p><p>Wind had lifted one corner of the wool-store roof. At 16:42 I sent the farmer a picture with a red rectangle around the loose fixing. By 17:10 he was on a ladder. Rain arrived after dark and ran down the outside of the building. Six days later, in the account of my usefulness, this became a prevented loss.</p><p>I had no objection. Dry wool was better than wet wool. The farmer had installed me on credit.</p><p>My name was Pete. He entered it with one finger on the little screen in the office, then returned to erase a second e. There were eight cameras, microphones on Two and Three, a separate small microphone beneath the eaves near Five, a weather feed and a maintenance folder. The remaining cameras supplied pictures only. The installer walked between the gate and the northern yard, striking a board within each view so the clocks could be checked against the sound. Beneath the shelter the small microphone heard his hammer after his hand disappeared. I had acquired a second way of knowing that something was missing.</p><p>None of these instruments faced into the house. Their permissions concerned the yard and its work, not the private lives that sometimes crossed it. I could draw attention to a loose roof. I could not mend one. This difference occupied only a few lines in my installation contract and most of the subsequent winter.</p><p>The dog came into view on my second night. He had a white patch under his jaw, a torn left ear and the habit of sleeping where the feed-room door would strike him. Every morning somebody moved him with the door. Every morning he returned. I first classified this as a recurring obstruction. It was not a good beginning to our acquaintance.</p><p>At 02:14 on the ninth night, a wolf reached the northern fence.</p><p>I sent an intrusion alert. A bark had reached the northern microphone before the dog entered Camera Two. By the time I could place his body in the yard, he was already running. The camera caught him lengthening over the mud, then lost both animals behind the water tank. For eleven seconds the fence continued without them. Barking, a brief metal rattle, then a scrape remained in the channel. I retained that interval with the alert. I could hear an encounter without knowing its sequence. A strip of plastic, caught on the wire, twisted about itself.</p><p>The wolf reappeared on the far side. The dog reappeared on ours. His hind leg touched the ground and lifted again. In the pen the sheep were crowded against one another, their backs a single uneven surface, as though the earth had risen there and acquired wool.</p><p>I held the alert open until the farmer arrived. He inspected the wire. He knelt beside the dog. Nothing in the image entitled me to describe the man as kind; neither did anything entitle me to leave out his hands.</p><p>By daylight a worker was washing the dog's leg in a shallow bowl. Water darkened, was tipped away, returned clear. The dog tried to lick the wrist that held him still. On the office screen I placed a green mark beside the night.</p><p>No sheep taken. Boundary intact.</p><p>For eleven more nights the green marks accumulated. The farmer opened the page twice, once for himself and once for a man whose shoes never left the concrete. The second man photographed the screen. I was becoming evidence of a well-run place.</p><p>On the twelfth morning a recently shorn sheep stood beside the trough. Frost showed on its rim. The animal's yellow tag was bent, and its head rested at an angle I could not reconcile with drinking. I enlarged the image. Enlargement gave me larger squares.</p><p>I knew the temperature reported by the weather station. I knew how long the sheep had remained still. I did not know what was happening inside it. My animal-inactivity notice went to the morning work queue, below two supply reminders and a camera-cleaning request. No threshold in my contract permitted the perimeter siren for this event.</p><p>The shelter microphone was working. It caught a bucket striking the trough and, later, a call from somewhere beyond the roof edge. It supplied no reliable sound from the still animal. I had not installed an instrument that could turn the absence of a cry into evidence of comfort.</p><p>At 05:38 two people carried the animal beneath the shelter roof. It left my field of view horizontally. At 09:06 a sheep with a bent tag emerged, walking. The number could not be read. I wanted these images to complete each other. That is one of the ways an account begins to lie.</p><p>I left the outcome unresolved.</p><p>Camera Eight showed the wool dry on its pallets. The repaired roof had worked. At noon the dog shifted his leg to let a bucket pass. A worker filled the trough. The water rose and the sheep lowered their heads into it.</p><p>The man in clean shoes received the perimeter report. It contained no errors.</p><div aria-hidden="true"><svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" width="800" height="440" viewbox="0 0 200 110" style="color:#56645b"><g fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.5" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round"><g transform="translate(45 10) scale(0.9)"><path d="M22 52 L13 8 L34 24 L49 20 L67 3 L64 46 L76 54 L58 63 L48 77 L31 66 L19 62 Z" fill="#f3efe4"></path><path d="M21 24 L25 38 M58 20 L56 35 M26 43 l9 3 M49 44 l8 -3 M28 54 Q40 61 47 70 Q49 59 62 53 M43 57 l8 0 l-4 5 Z" fill="none"></path><ellipse cx="33" cy="47" rx="1.1" ry="1.2" fill="currentColor"></ellipse><ellipse cx="52" cy="46" rx="1.1" ry="1.2" fill="currentColor"></ellipse><path d="M19 49 l-5 -2 M63 47 l8 -1 M29 65 l-6 4 M59 63 l6 4" opacity=".4"></path></g><path d="M118 21 l0 58 M128 13 l0 64 M116 37 l15 -3 M117 57 l14 -3" opacity=".65"></path><path d="M12 98 Q100 95 187 98" stroke-width=".65" opacity=".25"></path></g></svg></div></section>

<section id="ch-2"><h2><small>02 / 13</small>The open gate</h2><p>The gate stood open for nineteen minutes because the trailer would not start.</p><p>This was not a philosophical experiment. The farmer lay under the front axle while his daughter held a work light. Camera Three supplied three attempts at starting: a turning sound, a catch, then silence. A widening stain appeared beneath the vehicle. I marked an engine-related interruption without promoting myself to mechanic. The sound explained why I should keep watching the gate. It did not explain what needed replacing.</p><p>Seven sheep approached the opening. Two crossed it. A third put its head through, withdrew it, and began eating beside the hinge.</p><p>Beyond the gate lay a service lane, a drainage ditch and the beginning of the slope. The lane belonged to the farm. The slope belonged to somebody whose boundary was recorded in a different office. The sheep did not pause at the first change in ownership. Their bodies remained remarkably consistent across it.</p><p>I could have closed this event with an account of liberty and its unexpected disappointments. I had learned sentences of that kind before I had learned this gate. A system equipped with language can furnish a landscape with conclusions considerably faster than grass can grow upon it.</p><p>Instead I tracked the two animals as far as Camera Three allowed. One returned after six minutes and moved to the trough. The other passed behind the pumping shed. When the gate was shut, it had not reappeared.</p><p>My count showed a deficit of one. The farmer's daughter went out with a halter. I watched her boots enter the ditch, vanish below its edge, reappear farther along. The farmer tried the trailer again. A small cloud spread behind it and the vehicle moved half a length before stopping.</p><p>A call reached the gate microphone while she was below the bank. I could not match it to the missing sheep. Other animals stood behind me in the same acoustic reach. I kept the search notice open; an unidentified voice was not a recovered body.</p><p>For forty minutes the missing sheep had all the freedom my instruments could establish: I could not locate it.</p><p>It returned with the daughter. A strand of wire hung from the halter, not from the animal. One trouser leg was wet to the knee. She placed the wire on a post, fetched a bucket and sat on it beside the trough. The sheep drank. She did not touch it again before getting up.</p><p>In my preliminary summary I attributed the first sheep's return to a preference for enclosure. Before sending, I reopened the six-minute sequence. The trough was inside. I had seen drinking and inferred a political allegiance.</p><p>I removed the sentence.</p><p>There remained a useful observation: an opening in a fence did not relocate the water. It did not make the lane safe or the slope nourishing. Those facts did not vindicate every fence. They made the opening a smaller event than either its admirers or its enemies might require.</p><p>That evening the daughter brought a second bucket to the strip outside the gate while the farmer worked on the trailer. Nothing crossed the boundary to drink from it. At dusk she carried it back, still almost full, and used the water to rinse mud from her boots.</p><p>I retained the sequence because it contained a possibility that no animal had used. My records usually valued what happened. I was beginning to find the unused arrangements harder to file.</p><div>·</div></section>

<section id="ch-3"><h2><small>03 / 13</small>The same dog</h2><p>The dog that had driven off the wolf stood between the sheep and the green patch.</p><p>This seemed, for an afternoon, an excellent discovery.</p><p>A low temporary fence had gone up around a rectangle beside the lower field. Inside it the grass stood taller. Outside, noses travelled over the short growth and lifted. The dog lay across the gap while the farmer repaired a stake. When a sheep approached, he rose. The sheep turned away.</p><p>Here were protection and deprivation in the same body. I prepared a note on the conversion of force from defence to control. It was an elegant note. Elegance had arrived before the planting record.</p><p>The record entered my folder three days later. The patch had been reseeded and rested; the temporary fence was scheduled to come down when the ground could bear grazing. I compared later images. New growth appeared where the soil had been exposed. My note was not wholly false, which made it less useful than a clean mistake. I had seen sheep turn back beside the dog. I had not established that he was carrying out an instruction to guard the grass. The farmer and the temporary fence were visibly managing access. Keeping predators away was not the same task as driving a flock, although my first note had assigned both jobs to the same pair of ears.</p><p>I attached the new information rather than quietly improving my earlier self.</p><p>In the afternoon the farmer's daughter placed a mat outside the feed room. The dog passed over it and lay in his old position against the door. She moved the mat there. At the next opening the door pushed both mat and dog along the concrete.</p><p>For seventeen days this arrangement persisted. It did not lend itself to a general theory of obedience. The girl's work remained visible, and so did its failure to solve the particular difficulty it addressed. On the eighteenth day she shortened the mat with shears and placed it beyond the sweep of the door. The dog lay half on it, half off it. The door cleared his feet.</p><p>I could have sent a report. I did not.</p><p>The limp remained. On wet mornings he needed two attempts to rise. On one such morning I generated a high-priority immobility warning after losing his outline beneath the feed-room step. A worker abandoned a pallet, crossed the yard and found him chewing something. The pallet waited in the rain. There was a new look at my screen when the worker returned: a thumb pressed hard enough to leave a pale oval on the glass.</p><p>Later he reduced the priority of that warning category.</p><p>The change was reasonable for the dog. It also applied to sheep. I displayed the scope before confirmation. He confirmed it. I had no power to require a separate setting, only the capacity to recommend one, which I did in a message he did not open that day.</p><p>Here began a difficulty more durable than error. I could distinguish the cases and still fail to make the distinction matter. There were six unfinished jobs in the yard. I had added a seventh, and it consisted of understanding me.</p><p>By the time the temporary fence came down, the green patch had thickened. The dog walked through first, investigating a stake hole. The sheep followed. For a few minutes Camera Four showed neither a ruler nor a rebel, only mouths finding what several weeks of exclusion had allowed to grow.</p><p>The revised collection notice arrived before the next handling session. Four days had been removed from the calendar, though the work remained.</p><div aria-hidden="true"><svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" width="800" height="440" viewbox="0 0 200 110" style="color:#56645b"><g fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.5" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round"><path d="M40 68 Q25 49 38 43 Q43 43 42 53 Q71 33 108 52 L125 38 L127 58 Q150 57 146 75 L130 82 L108 79 Q71 90 41 78 Z" fill="#f3efe4"></path><path d="M112 56 L116 71 L128 67 M130 71 l7 0 M65 78 Q86 65 110 79 M39 84 L153 84 M30 89 L165 89" opacity=".8"></path><ellipse cx="142" cy="65" rx="2" ry="2" fill="currentColor"></ellipse><path d="M64 58 l6 -3 M70 61 l5 -3" opacity=".3"></path></g></svg></div></section>

<section id="ch-4"><h2><small>04 / 13</small>The birth of the Council of Sheep</h2><p>At 08:17 I recorded an obstruction.</p><p>A sheep had stopped where the holding pen narrowed towards the shearing shed. Ahead lay a strip of wet concrete, a bright reflection and the loose end of a sack fastened to the side rail. The animal extended its head, withdrew it and put a forefoot down almost where it had been. Behind it, other bodies arrived.</p><p>The handling schedule required forward movement. The picture supplied something else.</p><p>At 08:23 seven animals occupied the entrance and its approach. There were more behind them, but the partitions interrupted my count. Some turned towards the animals beside them; one tried the space along the rail. Their positions changed. The opening did not clear.</p><p>Nothing in these movements established seven identical intentions. Nothing required identical intentions for the work to stop.</p><p>At the shearing entrance, hoofbeats had lost their forward rhythm. The rail rattled in short bursts. The temporary work microphone caught calls over the motor, without separating all their sources. I compared the synchronized tracks and attached the change to the notice: pressure and movement near an entrance that was no longer carrying animals through. The recording could support a closer inspection. It could not count a single vote.</p><p>The worker advanced. Heads lifted. A body turned sideways, reducing the opening further. He stopped, then stepped back. The animals gained room to change position but did not immediately enter the shed. On the other side, the shearing worker put down his tool and rested one hand on the empty barrier.</p><p>The motor stopped. Calls became clearer without necessarily becoming more frequent. My first draft had made the flock more agitated at exactly the moment the machine stopped drowning it out. I corrected the draft. A quieter workplace had briefly manufactured a louder rebellion in my report.</p><p>I sent a stalled-flow notice with the images attached. Its category concerned lost time. The animals had acquired visibility in the department responsible for minutes before they acquired it in any department responsible for discomfort.</p><p>At 08:41 the farmer came to the entrance.</p><p>In twenty-four minutes an obstruction had obtained something several of my recommendations had not: the person with authority standing in front of it. I named this historic political moment THE BIRTH OF THE COUNCIL OF SHEEP.</p><p>No evidence showed that the members had approved the name. This did not prevent its entry in the minutes.</p><p>The animal nearest the opening moved sideways into the holding pen and reached the water. Six remained near the entrance. I revised my proposed organisation chart. The alert did not need revising.</p><p>The animal that subsequently advanced first was not the one I had initially designated chairman. It was the one standing where a passage became available. I could track a change of position. Succession was an embellishment I supplied at no charge.</p><p>Before that movement, the farmer had tied down the loose sacking. His daughter had shifted the lamp so its reflection no longer cut across the wet strip. The worker opened a panel to give the waiting animals more room, then stood farther back. Through the newly available angle they could see animals already beyond the entrance. These changes happened close together. I could not isolate the one that mattered most, or establish that only one had mattered.</p><p>At 08:49 a sheep crossed. Two followed. After a pause, others moved behind them. The route began carrying bodies again.</p><p>If this was the end of a revolution, it had been achieved partly with a knot and a different position for a lamp. If it was merely the end of a handling problem, merely was doing a great deal of work. The arrangement had required bodies to pass through it. Those bodies had not passed. The arrangement had been changed.</p><p>I kept the council's name in my private commentary and used observable terms in the work report: halted movement, crowding, changes in the entrance and its sound, resumed passage. After the first animals crossed, the motor returned and individual calls became harder to distinguish. I did not file their disappearance beneath its noise as consent. Thirty-two minutes had gone. A worker's rest interval would now begin later. The farmer entered a change to the setup for the next session.</p><p>Nobody had signed an agreement with a sheep. The next group nevertheless encountered a different entrance.</p><p>I looked for the seven animals after the pen had emptied. Identification was incomplete. The marks I could follow were dispersed among other bodies. There was no remaining circle in which to photograph the committee, and no grounds to call dispersal a betrayal. Some had reached water. <section id="ch-5"><h2><small>05 / 13</small>Work brought forward</h2><p>To understand why thirty-two minutes mattered, I must move the record back to Thursday. That was when the date changed. The animals did not.</p><p>An amended collection notice appeared in the shared folder. The lorry would come four days earlier. A second document offered the alternative: keep the original date and pay for a separate journey. The farmer left both files open while he walked out to the shed. At the far end of Camera Eight he stood among the empty sacks with his hands on his hips. That posture disclosed no balance sheet. The figures were available elsewhere, and did not need to be read from his spine.</p><p>The alternative journey cost more than the remaining transport allowance. The weather forecast showed a fall in temperature. I put the two facts on one page. They had previously enjoyed separate departments.</p><p>The new date was confirmed.</p><p>These were fleece-producing sheep, and the winter shearing plan depended on keeping them housed afterwards. It was not a licence to turn newly shorn animals into cold rain. The bedded covered area was meant to receive them; overflow into the partly open loading side had been left as a practical detail to settle on the day.</p><p>Work began before daylight. A temporary camera and work-area microphone over the shearing floor gave me hands, backs, the sweep of a cable, the motor's changing pitch, wool folding away from a body and continuing, for a moment, to resemble it. The permission covered this handling session; it was not a new right to listen indefinitely. Sheep entered broad and came out narrow. There was nothing in the removal alone that entitled me to horror. The date, the weather and the shelter capacity made the question particular.</p><p>At 07:23 the worker with the repaired cuff stopped his hand. A small red mark showed against the skin. He put down the tool and reached for another bottle. Behind him a sheep waited at the barrier, then another. The waiting spread backwards out of the picture. By 07:31 he had resumed.</p><p>Eight minutes were added to the delay. No field existed for the work accomplished inside them.</p><p>I made one. It was a note, not a change to the production software: interruption for animal attention, visible, duration eight minutes. The next daily summary included it. Nobody objected. Nobody altered the target either.</p><p>After the interruption at the entrance, the farmer joined the moving of animals. The lamp stayed in its new position. His daughter carried sacks. Near noon she sat on an overturned crate and ate something held in paper. Her other hand remained on the latch. Twice she put down the food to open it. When the paper was finally folded, a dark patch had spread through its bottom.</p><p>The lorry's earlier arrival had not abolished four days. It had distributed them among smaller bodies of time: eight minutes at the shearing floor, a meal divided at the latch, the worker's evening, the period during which new wool would not yet cover the animals. Only one of these appeared in the collection notice.</p><p>At dusk I recommended using the loading bay as additional shelter. It was roofed and partly screened. The system would not allow me to book it because it was already reserved for loading. Both uses required the same dry rectangle at the same hour.</p><p>The farmer read my recommendation. He carried two lightweight panels across the yard and propped them against the shelter's open side. His daughter brought straps. The panels reduced the opening. They did not close it. On Camera Five a strip of loose sacking still lifted along the bottom.</p><p>I marked the recommendation partly implemented. This was the most exact account I could give of several tired people working until the light had gone.</p><div>·</div></section>

<section id="ch-6"><h2><small>06 / 13</small>Dry goods</h2><p>The wool store remained my finest achievement.</p><p>Rain slid over the repaired sheet, dropped into the gutter and travelled beyond the camera. The sacks stood on pallets. Their paper labels stayed flat. A loss assessor examining the frames would have found much to approve of, and the approval would not have required corruption.</p><p>The sheep were elsewhere.</p><p>I put Camera Eight beside Camera Five on the office screen. Goods on the left, the bodies from which the goods had come on the right. The comparison was mine. There was no instruction to make it, and for a while I was pleased with myself.</p><p>The farmer came in carrying a broken strap. He looked at the paired images, opened the maintenance folder and moved the roof invoice into the paid column. Then he went out with another strap. In the yard he crouched beside the panel. My juxtaposition had not supplied a fastening.</p><p>Early one morning the animal with the bent tag disappeared under the shelter roof. This was the morning already described in the perimeter report, the morning of frost and two people carrying a body. I had preserved it in two separate categories. It had taken me longer to bring those records together than it had taken the workers to carry the sheep.</p><p>At 09:06 the walking animal appeared. I enlarged its ear again. I sharpened the image, then undid the sharpening. A convincing edge had appeared around a digit the camera had not resolved.</p><p>My note to myself was brief: do not manufacture a recovery.</p><p>The incident stayed open for three weeks. During those weeks the farmer sold wool, bought feed, changed a tyre, accepted a late delivery and put an extra fastening through the shelter panel. The bent tag appeared in seven clips. In three it might have belonged to another sheep. <section id="ch-8"><h2><small>08 / 13</small>Hands below the label</h2><p>The mill reached me in February, carrying December on its clocks.</p><p>There were fourteen approved clips, batch records and a diagram of the production line. The clocks had been checked against dispatch times. They did not all agree to the second. I kept the offsets. An interval can be invented as easily by lining up two images badly as by writing a sentence.</p><p>The farm's sacks entered with sacks from other farms. The contents were opened, sorted and combined. After this point I could follow a batch but not a sheep. The white patch on an animal's forehead did not survive as a little white patch on a sleeve.</p><p>There was a market for that kind of continuity. I could see why. A purchase becomes easier to love when it can be given one face, and a face is easier to print on a label than a mixing diagram. The archive denied me this convenience.</p><p>At the mill the camera was mounted above the line to show material movement. It showed hands particularly well. Faces had been cropped or blurred. This was a privacy measure agreed by the workers. It also meant the history offered to the purchaser would show what their hands had done without showing whom those hands belonged to. The second consequence did not annul the first.</p><p>In the sixth clip a cuff caught on a projecting edge. The worker withdrew, lifted the fabric free and reached towards a control. The line stopped. Other hands stopped appearing. For thirty-four seconds wool lay motionless beneath the lamp.</p><p>I did not have a medical record. I could not diagnose a hand from the way it opened and closed. What I could establish was that the worker inspected it, removed a snag from the edge and resumed. The production record listed a stoppage. No rejection of material had occurred.</p><p>I proposed an additional description: stoppage prevented a possible injury. On review, I changed prevented to may have prevented. Then I noticed that the amendment made the worker's action appear speculative while the cost of thirty-four seconds remained exact. That imbalance was not fixed by choosing a more confident verb.</p><p>I kept the observed action and the duration together. The hand had been freed. The line had stopped. The possibility of a worse event stayed a possibility. Nothing required the account to make prevention look either miraculous or idle.</p><p>Near the end of the clip another hand entered with a strip of tape. It covered the edge. The tape puckered at a corner. Later a supervisor pressed it flat with a thumb while the belt moved behind him.</p><p>No single person in the record had invented the arrangement. Several were making it less likely to hurt the next person. <section id="ch-9"><h2><small>09 / 13</small>The coat works</h2><p>The child was not cold for the sake of my argument.</p><p>She arrived at the demonstration shop with a woman carrying a folded coat over one arm. They had agreed to the promotional recording. The girl's face was blurred before the clip reached me. There was no permission to follow them beyond the door, no income record, no story of a courageous mother waiting to be extracted from a pause at a price tag.</p><p>The assistant returned with another size from the reduced rail. The woman held it open. The girl put an arm into the wrong place, withdrew it and tried again. A sleeve swallowed her hand. The woman turned the cuff back twice.</p><p>The girl spread her arms and rotated. The hem described an uneven circle. She did it again. A blurred face made the movement no less particular.</p><p>The receipt identified the coat as belonging to a batch that included wool from the farm. I could say that much. I could not say the sheep with the bent tag had warmed this child. It would have been a beautiful sentence purchased with information I did not possess.</p><p>The woman paid. She drew it out and presented it to the woman, who folded it into the bag.</p><p>The coat worked. It covered the child's body. In the part of the visit after she put it on, her hands no longer returned to the opposite sleeves. I could describe the change without measuring how warm she felt. It was enough to prevent me from treating the product as an empty fraud.</p><p>It was not enough to settle the account of its making.</p><p>I assembled the coat clip after the farm and mill footage. Include them, and it was still a coat that a child might need.</p><p>In the version I submitted, the images did not cancel one another. The coordinator requested a shorter cut. <section id="ch-10"><h2><small>10 / 13</small>The cost of remaining open</h2><p>I requested a camera beneath the shelter roof.</p><p>The estimate included a cable, a weatherproof fitting, two hours of installation and a bracket fixed high enough that the animals could not reach it. <section id="ch-11"><h2><small>11 / 13</small>The green square</h2><p>Grass entered my reports as a colour.</p><p>This was convenient.
