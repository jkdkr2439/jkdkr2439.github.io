You take one bite of beef and say, “This cow is tough.”

Slow down. The thing in your mouth is one piece. It came from one part of one
animal. Someone cut it, stored it, seasoned it, and cooked it. The cook may
have burned it. The knife may have gone the wrong way across the grain. Your
tooth may hurt.

Yet the mouth loves to rule. It gets one bite and passes judgment on the cow.

The mind does this all day. It hears one line from a fight and chooses the bad
person. It meets someone when they are tired and names their character. It
sees one number and calls it the whole state of a country.

Your mouth is a door. It is not a warehouse large enough for the world.

This does not mean the bite tells you nothing. It may truly be tough. If you
know where it came from and how it was cooked, you may learn more. A trace can
carry real news without being the whole thing.

The trouble begins when the size of the claim grows far beyond the size of the
contact.

Before you say, “This is how it is,” ask:

1. What part did I meet?
2. Through what door did it reach me?
3. What could that door not take in?
4. What did I add while making sense of it?
5. What else could leave the same trace?
6. What would help me tell those causes apart?
7. Is my sentence bigger than my bite?

You do not need to whisper every claim. You only need to pay for the distance
it travels.

One bite may support a claim about one bite. To judge the whole cow, bring
more than your mouth.
