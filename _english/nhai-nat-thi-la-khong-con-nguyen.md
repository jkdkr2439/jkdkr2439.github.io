You cannot swallow a large leaf as it is. You bite it. You crush it. You turn
its many edges into one soft lump.

That loss is useful. Without it, the meal may stop in your throat.

The mind also makes lumps. Many people become “family.” Many acts become
“kindness.” Millions of exchanges become “the market.” A small word lets us
carry a large field.

But the word carries less than the field. Some families share blood. Some
share a house. Some share only a long habit of caring. If you forget what was
crushed to make the word, you may force unlike things into one mouthful.

Making a thing smaller is not lying. A map must leave out trees to show roads.
A recipe must leave out the cook's childhood. The question is whether the
missing parts matter for the job at hand.

Before using a large word, ask what it has chewed together. Which differences
did it remove? Would one of those differences change the answer? Does the lump
still work when moved to another meal?

Use words. You cannot think without making manageable pieces. Just do not
expect the original leaf to remain whole inside the lump.
