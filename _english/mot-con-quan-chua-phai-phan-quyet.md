Your stomach turns once. Will you need the toilet in five minutes?

Maybe.

That answer annoys people because they want two boxes: yes or no. The body
does not owe them two boxes. A cramp can make one outcome more likely without
making it certain.

Belief has weight. You can lean a little, lean hard, or place your whole body
on a claim. New signs should change how much weight you place there.

One cramp makes the toilet possible. A second cramp, cold sweat, and the same
feeling you had during yesterday's illness may make it more likely. Seeing
that everyone who ate one dish is now running down the hall changes the weight
again. No single step must jump from nothing to certainty.

The movement can go the other way. Ten quiet minutes, a harmless cause, or a
test that points elsewhere can lower your trust. Changing the weight is not
betrayal. It is what the new sign is for.

Two people can agree on every fact and still place different weight on a
claim. Then the useful task is to find which step or earlier expectation makes
their scales differ.

This does not mean adding “maybe” to every sentence. Fog is not honesty. Say
where the doubt lives. Perhaps the sign is weak. Perhaps two causes remain.
Perhaps the tool is poor. Perhaps the cost of waiting is low.

A useful judgment tells you both direction and strength: what seems more
likely, how strongly, and what new event would move that level.

When you cannot be certain, do not pretend to be empty. Put the right amount
of weight on the floor you have.
