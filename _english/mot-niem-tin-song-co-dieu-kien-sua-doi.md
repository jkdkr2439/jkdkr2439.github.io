Before opening an envelope, a person announces: “If the result favors me, it
proves the test works. If it does not, it proves the test was rigged.”

The envelope has become ceremonial. No possible content can reach the belief.

> A belief remains part of inquiry only while some possible experience can
> alter its confidence, scope, mechanism, or use.

## Revision need not mean abandonment

When evidence conflicts with a claim, several responses are rational. The
claim may be rejected, narrowed, assigned lower confidence, or repaired by
changing an auxiliary assumption. A failed prediction does not logically
identify which component failed.

This flexibility is necessary. Instruments malfunction. Samples mislead.
Models improve by distinguishing conditions under which an effect appears.

The problem is not repair. It is repair without independent cost.

## Protective reinterpretation

A system protects itself when every outcome is translated into confirmation.
Success proves the theory. Failure proves sabotage. Silence proves secrecy.
Criticism proves fear of the truth.

Such reinterpretation may be emotionally satisfying because it converts danger
into fuel. Epistemically, it destroys discrimination. A claim compatible with
every outcome predicts nothing in particular.

## State revision conditions in advance

Before inspecting a disputed result, write:

- what outcomes are expected;
- which outcomes would be surprising;
- what would lower confidence;
- what would narrow the claim;
- which auxiliary assumptions may legitimately be checked;
- how many repairs are permitted before the core model is reconsidered.

Advance specification does not eliminate judgment. It makes later judgment
answerable to a prior record.

## Strong claims should risk more

A vague claim survives easily because it excludes little. A strong claim gains
informativeness by ruling out possible observations. That risk is part of its
value.

But falsifiability should not be made into a crude idol. Some hypotheses meet
evidence through complex models, and one failed observation may not settle
them. The deeper principle is exposure: the system must permit error signals to
travel inward.

## Correction should be recorded

Revision is often hidden because status rewards uninterrupted certainty. A
corrective culture preserves versions, failed predictions, retractions, and
changed confidence. The record makes learning visible.

A person who changes position under good evidence has not necessarily shown
weakness. A person who never changes may have achieved consistency by avoiding
contact.

## Revision has more than one depth

A belief can change at several layers. Confidence may fall while the claim
remains. Scope may narrow from “always” to “under these conditions.” A causal
mechanism may be replaced while the observed pattern survives. A practical
policy may change because costs changed even though the factual estimate did
not.

These layers should be named. Otherwise every modification is advertised as
vindication and every critic demands total surrender. One side protects the
castle by moving a chair; the other insists that a cracked window requires
demolishing the hill.

Good revision preserves what the evidence still supports and exposes what it
no longer does.

## Surprise is an epistemic asset

A system that never experiences surprise may be extraordinarily accurate. It
may also have become skilled at forgetting its expectations.

Record surprise before explanation domesticates it. What did the model lead us
to expect? Which result arrived instead? How much repair was required? This
brief interval between failed expectation and restored coherence is one of the
few places where a system can observe its own limits directly.

Closure begins when the interval becomes too embarrassing to preserve.

## A revision audit

1. What result would reduce confidence?
2. Was that condition stated before the result was known?
3. Which auxiliary assumption is being repaired?
4. Does the repair produce new independent predictions?
5. How many adverse outcomes has the system absorbed?
6. Is the claim becoming narrower and more precise, or merely harder to touch?
7. Is the history of revision publicly recoverable?

One protected belief can remain local. A closed system goes further: it
organizes sources, concepts, and counterarguments so that only its own outputs
can return as inputs.
