You look down, see black waste, and accuse the neighbor who gave you soup.

That is fast work.

The color is something you saw. The neighbor's guilt is a road you built from
it. Many bridges sit between them. What did you eat? What medicine did you
take? Is there bleeding? When did each event happen? Was the bowl unsafe?

We cross such bridges all day. A friend is silent, so we say they are angry.
A price rises, so we choose a villain. A child fails, so we name laziness.
Sometimes the bridge is sound. Sometimes it hangs over nothing.

You cannot live without crossing beyond what you directly see. Smoke can lead
you to fire. Footprints can lead you to a person. The skill is not refusing to
infer. It is showing the steps.

Think of a wet kitchen floor. A pipe may leak. A child may have spilled water.
Someone may have just cleaned it. The puddle fits all three stories. Looking
under the sink helps with the first. Finding a cup helps with the second. The
smell of soap helps with the third. A useful next look is one that makes the
stories stop giving the same answer.

People often choose the story that already has a villain, because a villain
feels like an ending. But a satisfying ending is not another sign. Anger may
tell you which cause you hate. It does not tell you which cause happened.

Ask what other roads reach the same sign. Then seek a new sign that separates
them. If medicine and bleeding can both darken waste, find a check on which
they do not give the same answer.

A trace may point. It does not whisper the only story that could have made it.
