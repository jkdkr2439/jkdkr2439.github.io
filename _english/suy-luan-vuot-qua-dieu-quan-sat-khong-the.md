No one sees a cause as a separate object beside its effects. No one observes
every member of a population. No one watches tomorrow arrive in advance.
Knowledge nevertheless reaches causes, populations, and futures.

It does so by inference.

Inference is not one movement. Deduction, induction, abduction, analogy, and
probabilistic updating preserve and risk different things. Calling all of them
“logic” hides the location of possible error.

## Deduction preserves consequence

In a valid deductive argument, if the premises are true, the conclusion cannot
be false. Validity concerns structure, not the truth of premises. A flawless
deduction from a false premise can produce a false conclusion.

The audit is therefore divided: does the conclusion follow, and are the
premises warranted?

## Induction extends patterns

Induction moves from observed cases toward unobserved cases or general
regularities. Its strength depends on sampling, stability, variation, and the
relevance of observed cases.

Hume's pressure remains: past regularity does not deductively entail future
regularity. Yet life and inquiry cannot avoid projection. The practical
response is to make projection conditional, graded, and sensitive to broken
mechanisms.

## Abduction selects an explanation

Abduction asks which hypothesis would best explain the available traces. It is
indispensable in diagnosis, history, science, and ordinary life. It is also
vulnerable to a neglected possibility: the best explanation considered may
not be the best explanation available.

Good abduction therefore widens alternatives before ranking them. It considers
fit, simplicity, mechanism, independent support, predictive novelty, and the
cost of auxiliary assumptions.

## Analogy transfers structure

An analogy does not argue merely because two things resemble one another. It
argues that a relevant relation in one case may hold in another.

The surface can seduce. A state is not literally a family; a mind is not
literally a computer; information is not literally water. Such comparisons
can reveal structure, but they can also import authority, intention, scarcity,
or flow where those features do not belong.

An analogy should state the transferred relation and the point at which the
mapping breaks.

## Probabilistic reasoning ranks uncertainty

Probabilistic inference permits evidence to change confidence without
demanding certainty. It separates prior plausibility from the diagnostic force
of new evidence. This is especially important when a striking observation is
compatible with both a rare hypothesis and a common alternative.

Numbers do not remove judgment. Priors, models, likelihoods, and categories
must still be chosen and criticized.

## Inference chains multiply fragility

Long arguments often hide their weakest bridge. A conclusion may depend on a
measurement, a classification, a generalization, a causal model, and an
analogy. Strength at one step does not repair failure at another.

Write the chain. Mark each transition. Then ask what kind of inference it is
and what would make that kind fail.

## The bridge can hide inside grammar

Inference is not always announced by words such as *therefore*. It often hides
inside a noun, a tense, or a choice of subject. “The policy caused the decline”
contains a causal bridge. “The corrupt committee” contains a judgment about
motive or conduct. “She finally admitted” implies that the proposition was
true before she spoke.

Arguments become difficult to inspect when their bridges are mortared into
description. One useful exercise is to rewrite the sentence as separate
claims: what was observed, what was classified, what was inferred, and what
was evaluated. The prose becomes less graceful for a moment. The reasoning
becomes visible.

## Explanation pays rent in constraint

An explanation is not improved merely by containing more causes. A story can
fit every detail because it was tailored after the event. The stronger
explanation constrains expectation: it tells us what else should occur, what
should not occur, and under which conditions the pattern should change.

This is why a beautiful retrospective account may remain epistemically poor.
It has arranged the furniture after entering the room and then praised itself
for predicting where the chairs would be.

An explanation earns confidence when it survives cases not used to design it,
competes successfully with alternatives, and exposes some part of itself to
failure.

## An inference audit

1. Which conclusion extends beyond direct observation?
2. What type of inference performs the extension?
3. What premises and background assumptions are required?
4. Which alternatives were considered?
5. Where could sampling, invalidity, analogy, or model choice fail?
6. What new observation would discriminate among explanations?
7. How much confidence survives the weakest step?

Few people can personally inspect every premise on which they live. Most
inference chains enter through speech, records, and institutions. That fact
does not make knowledge defective. It makes testimony fundamental.
