You know that a restaurant in another city made people sick. You were not
there. You did not eat the food. You did not inspect the kitchen.

Someone told you.

Most of what you know arrives this way. Other people see, measure, remember,
write, copy, check, and pass things on. If you accepted only what touched your
own skin, your world would end at the edge of one short life.

Trusting others is not a weakness. Blind trust is. So is blind refusal.

Do not ask only whether you like the speaker. Follow the meal through the
kitchen. Who first saw the event? What could they see? Who recorded it? What
changed as it passed from mouth to mouth? Was there a check by someone who did
not need the same story to win?

A long chain is not always bad. Clean steps can make a report stronger than a
single proud witness. A short chain is not always good. Your closest friend
can still repeat a lie.

Now imagine ten people tell you the same restaurant is filthy. That sounds
like ten sources. Then you learn that nine copied one post written by the
tenth. You have ten mouths but one pair of eyes.

Independence matters. Reports that arise from separate visits can check one
another. Reports that drink from the same first claim mostly show how far the
claim travelled. Repetition can tell you what a group has heard without
telling you what happened in the kitchen.

The chain also has joints. A witness may be careful while the headline is not.
A study may be sound while the summary removes its limits. Trust should attach
to the working parts, not float over the whole chain like perfume.

You cannot smell every toilet in the world. You can learn to inspect the pipe
through which the news reached you.
