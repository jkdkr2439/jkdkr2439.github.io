Ladies and gentlemen,

The master of ceremonies has just introduced me as **LORD LUCIS AURELIAN DI NOCTIS**. It is a title so magnificent that, when pronounced slowly enough, it can make three generations of my ancestors respectable without obliging anyone to investigate what they did. You may call me Lucis. Titles resemble many philosophical systems: the longer they become, the more earnestly they hope nobody will ask what is inside.

I have come to congratulate you.

A glorious age is opening. Programmers have begun to worry, writers have begun negotiations with their own shadows, and philosophers, after centuries of being paid chiefly in wooden chairs, library dust, and the gratitude of students who no longer remember their names, are at last to become indispensable. Machines require ethics. Ethics require definitions. Definitions require philosophers. Never has the road to a throne been paved with so many abstract nouns.

Accept my congratulations upon your approaching coronation, you new kings of the country of the blind.

It is a prosperous country. There, a man who distinguishes two shades of darkness may found a school. One who perceives the outline of a chair writes an ontology of furniture. One who touches the armrest is invited to draft a constitution for the entire room. And whoever is bold enough to announce that he sees the light receives a grant to teach the others the correct method of closing their eyes.

The philosophers of the former age used to bore deeply into a tree. They examined its bark, named its grain, debated the moral qualities of sap, and followed one root underground. The labour began honourably. A tree deserves to be understood. But they dug so long that they forgot they were beneath a tree. At the thousandth root they declared that they had found the skeleton of the earth. When they struck stone, they called it the resistance of Being. When the spade broke, they wrote a treatise upon the necessary limits of instruments. Nobody thought of climbing back to the surface to see whether a forest stood nearby.

Artificial intelligence has now arrived to assist them.

It does not ask whether the pit was dug in the wrong place. It catalogues the pit. It installs electric lamps along the walls, numbers every stratum of mud, builds a library beneath the roots, and supplies seventeen objections to anyone who doubts that the cellar is the universe. From one uncertain premise it can construct a palace complete with columns, vaults, a coat of arms, and a bodyguard of footnotes. The philosopher surveys the splendour and feels relieved: an idea so richly furnished can scarcely be wrong.

Error used to dress very poorly. We have improved its circumstances.

You will tell me that the machine can argue against you. Of course it can. It may play a Kantian before breakfast, a consequentialist after luncheon, a neurologist before tea, and a ferocious sceptic precisely when dessert arrives. Four voices fill the chamber, while the key remains in one man's pocket. The actors change coats; the stage does not change. The premise chooses the room, the vocabulary raises its walls, the question locks the door, and the debate is praised as free because each prisoner may occupy a different chair.

It is a most elegant form of criticism. It never embarrasses the host.

Zeno once had to divide the racecourse into infinitely many pieces by himself. The work was exhausting; confusion still demanded labour in those days. The modern Zeno need only type a request. The machine will make further divisions, explain them, attack its own explanation, defend the attack, add a table, and recommend a more engaging title. The real Achilles passed the window some time ago. Nobody in the chamber noticed, for you were conducting a symposium upon the absolute impossibility of his arrival.

I do not blame Zeno. A beautiful paradox possesses its own seduction. I merely wonder why seduction is treated as a certificate in mechanics. A map may be lovelier than a territory. A portrait may be more charming than its sitter. A theory may even be more consistent than the world, for the world is so badly educated that it frequently contradicts itself. Yet when a bridge collapses, the stones will not read the commentary.

What delights me most about the new reign is not that you will possess greater power. Your power will wear the uniform of neutrality.

Formerly, a mistaken philosopher might inconvenience several generations of readers. To distribute his error, he required a printer, disciples, a university, and sometimes a king sufficiently bored to finance him. Error travelled on foot, grew old, and occasionally died upon the road. Today a definition may pass directly into training data, reward models, benchmarks, filters, hiring policies, and systems of decision. It need no longer persuade each person. It is executed.

You define harm, and a million doors learn how to close. You define safety, and a garden may be uprooted because its thorns offend someone. You define fairness, and the scales quietly inquire whose weight shall count, for how long, and in units minted by which household. Upon the interface the user sees a machine. Behind the machine, one of your adjectives is wearing a crown.

It is a beautiful metamorphosis: prejudice enters the machine in provincial clothes and emerges in the ceremonial dress of universal truth.

I know that you have brought deontology, consequentialism, virtue ethics, and principles enough to equip the sovereign of silicon. The armour is admirably bright. I am curious only about the miner who supplied the metal, the soldier who must wear it, and the child standing wherever the spear points. Every principle possesses a marble entrance and a back door reserved for the bill. Philosophy generally comes through the front; consequences have the domestic habit of arriving through the kitchen.

Who defines harm? Who is admitted to the sum of benefit? How many years must the future contain before today's agony may be reduced to a decimal? When the autonomy of one person becomes the inconvenience of a million, who exchanges them into the same currency? And when the model fails, who must inhabit the answer?

If the author of a definition is also exempt from its consequences, you do not possess an ethical principle. You possess a privilege that has learned to write footnotes.

Pray do not mistake my irreverence. I love philosophy. One should mock only what may still be saved; flowers are more suitable for what is entirely dead. Philosophy begins when a person distrusts the name he has just bestowed. It lives when an idea consents to leave the drawing room, pass through the factory, hospital, market, prison, and kitchen, and return with dirty shoes and several sentences that require correction. A concept that refuses friction resembles an aristocrat who refuses rain: its elegance is preserved because someone else carries the umbrella.

Business is no oracle, nor is programming. A merchant may price everything and lose all sense of value; an engineer may make a system travel perfectly toward a destination nobody should reach. Yet both retain one uncivil habit from which philosophers might profit: reality is permitted to refuse. The code fails. The money ends. The user evades the rule. The incentive changes direction. The edge case enters through the window and shatters the tea service. A beautiful model encounters an ugly no, and ugliness occasionally saves the intellect.

You are about to command a servant that never tires, has read thousands of books, can write thousands of pages, and can make any well resound like a cathedral. Beware the echo. It always answers in the voice of an abyss, even when the abyss is no deeper than the vanity leaning over it.

I propose a modest coronation rite. Before placing a crown upon a definition, compel it to name its parents: the culture that produced it, the purpose it serves, the resolution at which it operates, what it cuts away, and whom it leaves beyond the boundary. Make it identify a second frame of reference entitled to humiliate it. Give it a condition of failure, a method of correction, and a person responsible for opening the door when consequences knock at night.

If it cannot comply, do not destroy it. Feeble ideas deserve humanity. Place it in a museum beside perpetual-motion machines and aristocratic genealogies. The public may admire a beautiful design that once wished to govern the world but never learned how to descend a staircase.

My future kings, the new age does indeed belong to you. Humanity has never more urgently required people able to question premises, reopen concepts, and perceive the authority hidden inside a definition. Humanity has never faced greater danger from those who can perform those three acts only in beautiful sentences.

The country of the blind is preparing its throne. Artificial intelligence is polishing the crown. The crowd is ready to call eloquence understanding, provided the answer arrives quickly and appears to have read many books.

Proceed to the dais. I ask only that, before sitting, each of you look at the throne through the frame of reference of the person who will be crushed beneath it.

If after that minute you still desire to be king, at least you will know the weight of your sovereignty.
