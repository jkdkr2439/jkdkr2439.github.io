The whole village says the water is sweet. You lift the lid and smell rot.

Does the vote clean the well?

Many people agreeing can matter. Perhaps they tested the water in different
ways and reached the same result. Perhaps a practice survived because it kept
working. Agreement can collect many eyes and many trials.

But agreement can also collect one fear. Everyone may copy the same first
mistake. Everyone may depend on the man who owns the well. People may say
“sweet” because saying “rotten” costs them dinner.

There is a difference between fifty people tasting the water and fifty people
hearing the mayor describe it. There is also a difference between free praise
and praise given while the man who owns the well writes down names.

Sometimes agreement earns force because different hands, tools, and roads meet
at one result. Sometimes it only reveals who controls the road. The head count
looks the same. The making of the count does not.

A lone objection is not holy either. The person who cries “rotten” may want
the well for himself. Disagreement deserves inspection, not a crown. Test what
each side had access to and what each side could lose.

Count is therefore a clue, not a spell. Ask how the agreement was made. Did
people reach it through separate roads? Could they speak against it? Did the
claim survive contact with results, or only contact with punishment?

One lonely person can be wrong. A crowd can be wrong together. The world does
not change its smell to spare either side.

Listen to the village. Then inspect the well.
