Two people taste the same soup. One says it is bland. The other reaches for
water because it burns.

The soup did not split into two worlds. It met two mouths.

A mouth is not an empty hole. It has a tongue, teeth, wounds, habits, and a
history. A blocked nose steals part of the flavor. A sore tooth changes what
can be chewed. A child and a cook do not meet salt in the same way.

The world gives something. The body gives something. The taste happens in the
meeting.

The same is true when you look and listen. A frightened person sees a dog
differently from its owner. A mechanic hears a fault inside an engine where a
passenger hears noise. A hungry person notices bread before the color of the
plate.

This does not mean the soup is only in your head. Too much salt can still be
measured. Heat can still burn both mouths. The thing outside pushes back.

But you never meet it without a way of meeting it.

When two people report different experiences, do not rush to pick a side.
The world did not make all of it. The person did not invent all of it. Ask
what the world supplied, what each body received, and what happened at contact.

The useful question is not, “Which mouth owns the true soup?”

Ask instead:

- What stayed the same in the bowl?
- What differed in the mouths?
- Which difference can we test?
- What would another mouth or tool reveal?

You taste the meal. You do not receive it without yourself.
