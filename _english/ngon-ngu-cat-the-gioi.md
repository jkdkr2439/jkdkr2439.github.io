A coastline has no labels. A map places one name over the water, another over
the land, and a line between jurisdictions. The line can determine taxes,
rescue duties, fishing rights, and war. It is not painted upon the sea. It is
not therefore unreal.

Language cuts the world in two senses. It distinguishes, and it wounds.
Naming allows a phenomenon to become shareable, comparable, and contestable.
It can also harden a temporary division, conceal a continuum, or attach a
judgment before inquiry begins.

> Words do not create the whole world, but they organize what can be carried
> from one mind, record, and institution to another.

## Names are tools of recurrence

Without stable names, each inquiry would begin again from a mute particular.
A word allows scattered cases to be treated together. It supports memory,
coordination, law, measurement, and criticism.

But recurrence comes at a price. A word arrives with prior uses. It may carry
metaphors, emotional color, institutional rules, and inherited disputes. Two
people can agree on every observable detail and still quarrel because one
calls an action *discipline* while the other calls it *coercion*.

The first epistemic task is to discover whether the dispute concerns the event,
the classification, or the evaluation carried by the classification.

## Descriptions contain angles

No description includes every true feature. Selection begins before a
sentence is complete. “The police dispersed the crowd,” “the police cleared
the road,” and “the police broke up the protest” may refer to overlapping
events while assigning different agents, objects, purposes, and moral
expectations.

This does not make accurate description impossible. It makes comparison
necessary. Ask what each sentence foregrounds, suppresses, presupposes, and
allows the reader to infer.

## Definitions serve different jobs

A dictionary reports patterns of use. A technical definition stabilizes a
term for inquiry. A legal definition establishes consequences. An operational
definition specifies how something will be measured. These jobs may overlap,
but none automatically settles the others.

Many sterile arguments occur because a participant presents one kind of
definition as sovereign. A legal category is treated as a scientific kind. A
measurement rule is treated as the essence of a phenomenon. Popular use is
treated as proof that a technical distinction is fraudulent.

Definitions should be judged by the work they claim to perform.

## Euphemism and accusation

Language can hide agency. Passive construction makes an action appear to have
happened by weather: mistakes were made, lives were lost, data were exposed.
Language can also manufacture agency: a population is made to act through a
collective noun, or an outcome is attributed to intention without evidence.

Euphemism lowers emotional visibility. Accusatory language raises it. Either
may occasionally be accurate. Neither should be allowed to supply the missing
causal argument.

## Translation reveals hidden structure

A difficult translation often exposes what speakers of one language no longer
notice. A term may divide an emotional field differently, encode social rank,
or preserve an older image. Translation therefore cannot be simple word
replacement. It requires reconstructing the function of an expression within
its historical and local world.

The test of a translation is not whether every word has a partner. It is
whether relevant distinctions, force, ambiguity, and relation survive the
crossing.

## Metaphors import machinery

To call an argument a foundation suggests that later claims rest upon it. To
call memory a storehouse suggests that recollection retrieves a preserved
object. To call public discussion a marketplace suggests competition,
exchange, and eventual sorting by demand. Each metaphor illuminates by
importing a machine.

The imported machine has spare parts the speaker may never have intended.
Foundations are static. Storehouses keep objects unchanged. Markets reward
what is wanted, not necessarily what is true. A metaphor becomes dangerous
when its unexamined machinery begins making inferences on the speaker's behalf.

Do not forbid metaphor. Open it. Ask which relation is being transferred,
which additional relations arrived without permission, and where the mapping
breaks. Literal language cannot rescue us because literal descriptions also
select. The discipline is not wordlessness. It is custody.

## Precision can conceal as well as reveal

Technical vocabulary is justified when it preserves a distinction ordinary
language repeatedly loses. It becomes costume when a familiar confusion is
translated into expensive nouns and returned unchanged.

The test is severe: can the writer reconstruct the mechanism in ordinary
language without destroying it? If not, the term may name genuine complexity.
Or it may be a locked cabinet whose owner has misplaced the key. The reader
should not be asked to admire the cabinet.

## A language audit

For a disputed sentence, ask:

1. Which event or relation is being named?
2. Which alternatives could describe the same material?
3. What evaluation is smuggled into the label?
4. What agency is revealed, assigned, or hidden?
5. Which definition is operating, and for what purpose?
6. What would be lost if the sentence were translated into neutral operations?
7. What would neutralization itself conceal?

Language does not merely report evidence. It helps determine what will count
as a datum, a claim, and an alternative. We are therefore ready to examine the
relation most often invoked and least often reconstructed: evidence.
