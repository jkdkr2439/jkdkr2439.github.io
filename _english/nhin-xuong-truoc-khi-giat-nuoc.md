Most people want to flush at once. The thing is ugly. It smells. It came from
us, and we would rather be done with it.

But sometimes looking down is useful.

What left the body can show what the body did. It may reveal water, blood,
food not broken down, or a change that needs care. You do not worship the
waste. You inspect it, learn what you can, and change what enters next.

Your claims are also products. The world touched you. Your senses took a
part. Attention chose. Memory mixed. Words pressed. Reason joined missing
pieces. Then a sentence came out.

A mature mind does not love every sentence because it made it. Nor does it
hate itself when one sentence is wrong. It looks at the product and asks what
the process did.

Could I state the claim clearly? Did I use a word in two ways? Did I turn one
sign into one cause too quickly? Did all my sources drink from one pipe? What
would make me lower my trust? Who pays if I act and fail?

The aim is not to become a person with no firm beliefs. The aim is to build
beliefs that can meet the world again.

Being correct once may be luck. Being correct for long requires a way to find
error, survive finding it, and change.

So look down before you flush. Keep what the trace can teach. Let the rest go.
Then choose the next meal with a little more care.
