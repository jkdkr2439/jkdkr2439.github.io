**Text under criticism:** [Hội Đồng Cừu — *Nguyen Thanh Nam: A Political "Accident," or "Perpetual" Revolutions*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6cRdEg-IxUQ).

## 1. The argument deserves a stronger criticism than denial

The Hội Đồng Cừu video begins from an arrest it presents as paradoxical. The subject is not described as an external opponent of the political order, but as an admirer who wrote in praise of its central figure. The video then reconstructs a genealogy running through Robespierre, Lenin, Trotsky, Stalin, and Mao. Its proposed mechanism is intelligible: a revolution that cannot declare itself complete must continually reproduce the category of the enemy; a political field without neutral space requires permanent demonstrations of purity; and the least expensive way to demonstrate one's own purity is to identify impurity in another. Denunciation thereby ceases to be an accidental excess and becomes a recurrent mode of political reproduction.

That reconstruction contains a serious insight. It would be intellectually cheap to answer it by denying the historical occurrence of denunciation, the doctrinal production of enemy categories, or the conversion of vigilance into civic virtue. Robespierre did explicitly place virtue and terror together within revolutionary government, describing terror as severe and inflexible justice and as an emanation of virtue ([Robespierre 1794](https://www.marxists.org/history/france/revolution/robespierre/1794/political-morality.htm)). Lenin did write that there could be no neutral third ideology in a society divided by class antagonism and that deviation from socialist ideology strengthened bourgeois ideology ([Lenin 1902](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1901/witbd/ii.htm)). These are not inventions of the video.

The weakness lies elsewhere. The historical and textual evidence can establish occurrence, authorization, and a mechanism of reproduction. The video then asks that evidence to establish ontology. That last inference carries more weight than the evidence has yet earned.

## 2. The proposition on which the conclusion depends

Four propositions must be distinguished:

1. Denunciatory practices occurred repeatedly within certain revolutions.
2. Certain revolutionary institutions made those practices more likely and more durable.
3. Certain doctrines supplied vocabularies and justifications through which those practices could be normalized.
4. Denunciation is constitutive of revolution as such, or of the relevant class of left revolutions, such that it belongs to their political ontology.

The first proposition is historical. The second is causal-institutional. The third concerns doctrinal affordance and legitimation. The fourth is modal and constitutive. It does not merely say that denunciation occurred, recurred, or was justified. It says that denunciation belongs to what the political form is.

No accumulation of examples automatically converts the first three propositions into the fourth. Recurrence is compatible with essence, but it is not identical to essence. A feature may recur because several systems share contingent pressures, institutional technologies, wartime conditions, administrative weakness, elite incentives, or methods of mobilization. To call the feature constitutive, one must specify at least the relevant class, the necessity relation, and the counterexample rule. Would a revolution cease to be the relevant kind of revolution if it contained no public denunciatory machinery? What observation could count against the ontological thesis? If every counterexample is reclassified as an unfinished, betrayed, or non-genuine revolution, the thesis becomes protected by its own definitions.

This is the point at which the video's strongest empirical material meets its weakest conceptual bridge.

## 3. “Denunciation” names a formed action, not its generative depth

The word *đấu tố* arrives after a sequence of operations has already acquired a recognizable form. These operations may include public identification, accusation, compelled self-criticism, collective moral pressure, revocation of neutral status, exclusion, or punishment. Grouping them under one name is useful. It lets us compare cases. It does not yet identify the relation that makes the operations arise.

The analytical mistake is to treat the name of the output as the name of the generator.

To reach the generator, the action must be decomposed:

```text
a shared object, person, practice, or symbol
→ incompatible accounts of what it is and what counts as proper conduct toward it
→ different practical consequences licensed by those accounts
→ a conflict over which account governs the shared field
→ unequal authority to institutionalize one account
→ classification of rival users as mistaken, impure, disloyal, or dangerous
→ sanctions attached to rival use
→ public accusation rewarded as enforcement and as proof of allegiance
→ the formed action later named denunciation
```

The root is therefore not a mysterious appetite for denunciation contained inside a doctrine. The root is a conflict over definition that has become a conflict over the authority to make a definition binding.

This claim requires a qualification. Disagreement over definition is not sufficient for denunciation. Most disputes over words do not end in punishment. The transition requires further conditions: the disputed term must govern action; identity, legitimacy, property, or safety must be attached to its use; one party must possess organizational or coercive capacity; rival use must be sanctionable; accusation must be rewarded or protective; and false accusation must be relatively cheap. Definition conflict supplies the object of struggle. Power, incentives, and institutions supply the passage into coercion.

## 4. From disagreement about an object to disagreement about the rule of description

Philosophy of language offers a vocabulary for this shift, although the phenomenon does not depend on that vocabulary. W. B. Gallie's account of “essentially contested concepts” concerns evaluative concepts whose proper use remains subject to sustained dispute rather than simple empirical settlement ([Gallie 1956](https://academic.oup.com/aristotelian/article-abstract/56/1/167/1793543)). Not every politically disputed term meets Gallie's full conditions, so the category should not be used as a decorative citation. Its limited relevance is that terms such as democracy, justice, loyalty, or reverence can carry appraisive weight while remaining internally disputed.

David Plunkett and Timothy Sundell's work on metalinguistic negotiation identifies another useful distinction. Some exchanges that appear to concern whether an object falls under a word also function as disputes over how the word should be used in the relevant context, given the practical work that use will perform ([Plunkett and Sundell](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/items/8e67e7f3-9a77-4821-a803-b0611d1b03d1)). The speakers are not merely reporting private dictionaries. They are contesting which way of classifying should govern thought and action.

This distinction clarifies the apparent paradox at the center of the video. A writer may intend reverence. An institution may hold that only authorized forms of representation count as reverence. The conflict is not resolved by proving the sincerity of the writer's intention, because the disputed matter is precisely who possesses standing to determine the public criteria of reverence.

Three propositions that look similar must therefore be separated:

```text
I intended my work as reverence.

My work satisfies my criteria of reverence.

I possess authority to determine which forms will publicly count as reverence.
```

The first may be psychologically true while the third is institutionally denied. Once this distinction is visible, the case ceases to be paradoxical. An insider can be punished without first being reinterpreted as a secret outsider. The subject may remain sincere while being classified as unauthorized.

## 5. Definitional authority does not make a definition true

To say that power enters definition is not to say that truth is whatever power declares. That would replace one conceptual error with another. The narrower claim is institutional:

```text
definition
→ permitted inference
→ permitted action
→ classification of persons
→ allocation of sanction
```

Power cannot make an odor fragrant or a false statement true. It can determine which report enters the official record, which vocabulary is admissible, which inference an office may draw, and what cost attaches to refusal. Its effect is not semantic omnipotence. Its effect is the unequal distribution of consequences among competing descriptions.

This also explains why the use of the same word can conceal a deeper conflict. Both sides may say “reverence,” “loyalty,” “revolution,” or “enemy.” Their disagreement does not vanish because the visible label is shared. The label may be the site through which incompatible criteria compete.

At this level, denunciation is a technology of boundary enforcement. It publicly identifies a rival use, attaches that use to a defective person, and demonstrates the accuser's membership in the authorized community. The target's account of accusation as the cheapest proof of purity fits this model extremely well. But what it fits is a general enforcement mechanism, not yet an ontology exclusive to a revolutionary tradition.

## 6. What the revolutionary texts do prove

The primary texts matter because doctrines can alter the transition conditions.

Robespierre's articulation of revolutionary virtue and terror does more than describe violence. It redescribes coercive severity as a consequence of democratic virtue under revolutionary conditions. The conceptual movement matters: terror is not left outside morality as an unfortunate instrument; it is drawn into a morally authorized classification. That can lower resistance to coercive action by changing what the action counts as.

Lenin's rejection of a neutral third ideology likewise supplies a classificatory closure. If even slight deviation objectively strengthens the opposing ideology, inactivity or ambiguity can acquire partisan meaning without the subject intending it. The transcript correctly sees why this structure can place pressure on neutrality.

These texts may therefore do at least three kinds of work:

1. provide names for enemies;
2. convert ambiguous conduct into politically legible deviation;
3. transform vigilance and accusation into duties compatible with virtue.

That is already a strong conclusion. It is also narrower than the ontological conclusion. A doctrine can make a practice intelligible, permissible, or probable without making it a necessary constituent of every political formation categorized under that doctrine.

The distinction is not pedantic. It determines what kind of explanation is being offered. If denunciation is an essence, history merely reveals what the thing always was. If denunciation is an outcome generated under identifiable conditions, then variation matters, institutions matter, and interruption is conceptually possible.

## 7. The false binary: accident or ontology

The video's central question offers two options: denunciation is either an accident and deviation, or it belongs to the ontology of permanent revolution. This binary omits the largest explanatory field between them.

A phenomenon can be:

- structurally encouraged without being logically necessary;
- repeatedly reproduced without being eternal;
- doctrinally licensed without being uniquely entailed;
- institutionally stable without being metaphysically constitutive;
- difficult to abolish without being impossible to interrupt.

Calling such a phenomenon an “accident” understates it. Calling it an “ontology” overstates what has been shown. The appropriate category is a recurrent mechanism with specified enabling conditions.

The better question is therefore:

> Under what conditions does disagreement over definition become a system of public accusation and coercive correction?

This question preserves the video's historical evidence while allowing comparison across institutions and traditions. It also generates testable subquestions. Is the governing definition open to revision? Are accusers rewarded? What is the cost of a false accusation? Can silence remain neutral? Can an unauthorized interpretation remain mistaken without turning its author into an enemy? Does a new observation revise the classification, or does every result reconfirm it?

## 8. The strongest part of the video survives the criticism

A good criticism should not destroy the part of its target that deserves to live.

The video is right that a system can require continuous performances of purity. It is right that the identification of impurity elsewhere can become an efficient means of self-protection. It is right that institutions built from above may be fueled from below, and that the resulting machinery can turn upon its builders. It is also right that the disappearance of neutral space changes the meaning of silence.

The correction concerns explanatory depth. “No neutral space” is not the final root. Neutrality disappears because a governing definition has divided the field so that every position, including non-participation, receives partisan meaning. “Permanent enemy” is not the final root either. An enemy is produced when a rival description of reality is reclassified as a threat to the order authorized to define reality.

The historical tradition studied by the video may provide especially powerful forms of this mechanism. That comparative claim can be investigated. What cannot be assumed is that the historical concentration of a mechanism proves its exclusive or necessary residence there.

## 9. Conclusion

Denunciation is not the deepest object of explanation. It is the visible name of a completed action sequence.

The deeper sequence begins when several definitions of a shared object license incompatible ways of acting. It intensifies when the parties cease merely to disagree and begin to contest which definition will govern the common field. It becomes coercive when one party can attach sanctions to rival use. It becomes denunciatory when accusing another is rewarded both as enforcement and as evidence of one's own allegiance.

This model does not excuse denunciation. It explains why naming it as the ontology of a doctrine is analytically premature. The doctrinal genealogy in the video can show how revolutionary language closes neutrality, regenerates enemy categories, and authorizes coercive vigilance. It does not yet show that denunciation is necessary to revolution as revolution.

The decisive question is not whether the writer truly revered the historical figure. It is who possessed the authority to determine what could publicly count as reverence, and how far that authority could coerce rival definitions.

Denunciation begins when a community stops correcting propositions and starts using persons to defend the compulsory meaning of its words.

# THE HUMAN LANGUAGE

# Two Million Noses and One Piece of Shit

Let us put one thing on the table before someone pretends not to understand.

The piece of shit in this story is not Ho Chi Minh. It is not Nguyen Thanh Nam. It is not a revolution, a country, or a person.

It is only an object that is easy to smell.

We use it because a simple object makes one mechanism easy to see. The mechanism is how one person's way of naming something becomes a rule that every other person must obey.

## Two noses

There is a piece of shit in a room.

One man says, “It stinks.”

Another man says, “It smells wonderful.”

Nothing terrible has happened. One of them may be lying. One may have a broken nose. They may use the word “wonderful” in different ways. They can argue, laugh, test the smell, or leave.

This is only disagreement.

Now each man brings two thousand friends. Then two million. They bring signs, speakers, offices, rules, and people who can lock the door.

The question changes.

It is no longer:

> What does this thing smell like?

It becomes:

> Who gets to decide what everyone must call the smell?

That is a different fight.

## When a word starts giving orders

At first, the second man said:

> I smell something wonderful.

Now he says:

> This smell must be called wonderful.

Then:

> Anyone who calls it foul has a dirty nose.

Then:

> A dirty nose is dangerous to the room.

The shit has not changed. The nose has not changed. What changed is the price of using a word.

The man with the key cannot make the smell sweet. But he can decide which report goes into the book. He can decide who keeps a chair, who loses a job, and who leaves through the locked door.

Power does not need to rule the smell. It only needs to rule what happens to people who describe it.

## The cheapest way to prove your nose is clean

Soon everyone in the room must prove that their nose works properly.

How do you prove it?

You can give a long speech about the wonderful smell. But speeches take time. You can hold your nose and smile. But people may doubt you.

The cheapest proof is to point at someone else.

- That man made a face.
- That woman opened a window.
- That child said the room smells strange.

By finding a dirty nose, you make your own nose look clean.

Now accusation has two jobs. It protects the official word. It also protects the accuser.

Only now do we need the word *denunciation*.

Denunciation was not hiding inside the piece of shit. It appeared when one nose gained the power to correct every other nose, and when pointing at another person became the safest way to survive the correction.

## Why the loyal man can still be punished

Return to the harder case.

A writer says he meant to honor a historical figure. That may be true. He may be completely sincere.

But the people who guard the public meaning of “honor” can answer:

> You do not decide what counts as honor here.

The writer is talking about his intention. The institution is talking about its right to approve the form.

Those are not the same thing.

So there is no great mystery in a loyal person being punished. The system does not need to prove that he secretly hated the person he praised. It only needs to say that his way of praising was not his to define.

The fight is no longer over whether he meant respect. It is over who owns the public meaning of respect.

## Where the video is right

The video sees something real.

When nobody is allowed to stand outside the fight, silence looks guilty. When everyone must prove loyalty, accusing someone else becomes cheap protection. A machine built by leaders can be fed every day by ordinary people. It can even turn around and eat the people who built it.

All of that is worth keeping.

But the video starts too late and ends too far away.

It starts with the finished act, gives the act a name, and then looks for the political family that owns the name. It ends by calling the act part of the family's very being.

Go one floor lower.

Before accusation, there was a fight over what a word allowed people to do. Before the enemy, there was a person using the wrong meaning. Before the wrong meaning became a crime, someone gained the power to make one meaning compulsory.

That is the root.

## Accident or eternal nature?

Something does not have to be a small accident just because it is not eternal.

A kitchen can keep catching fire because the same pipe leaks beside the same stove. The fires are not random. The kitchen is badly built. But fire is still not the eternal soul of kitchens.

Find the pipe. Find the heat. Find the rule that stops anyone from turning off the gas.

The same goes here.

Public accusation can return again and again because a system keeps producing the same conditions:

- one meaning must rule;
- silence is given a hostile meaning;
- disagreement is attached to the character of the speaker;
- accusation earns safety;
- false accusation costs little;
- nobody can inspect the official nose.

That is a strong explanation. It is stronger than saying “a few bad people made a mistake.” It is also clearer than saying “the act lives forever inside the soul of revolution.”

## The question to carry home

Do not ask only:

> Is this person using the correct word?

Ask five questions, in this order:

1. Who chose the meaning of the word?
2. What actions does that meaning allow?
3. What happens to a person who uses another meaning?
4. Can the ruling meaning ever be corrected?
5. Who becomes safer by accusing someone else?

Two people can disagree about a smell and go home.

Denunciation begins when one of them owns the room, locks the door, writes the dictionary, and asks the crowd to find every nose that still works differently.
