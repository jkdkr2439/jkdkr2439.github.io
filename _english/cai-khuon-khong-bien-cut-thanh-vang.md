A shop presses cheap meat into the shape of a fine steak. The shape is neat.
The label is grand. The meat has not read the label.

Names help us cut up a busy world. One shelf becomes “food.” Another becomes
“poison.” One act becomes “care,” another “control.” These cuts help us move,
warn, remember, and argue.

Then the trick begins. We forget that a person made the cut. We treat the
label as if it grew on the thing.

Calling a beating “discipline” does not tell us what the blow did. Calling a
sale “charity” does not settle who gained. Calling a pile “gold” does not
change its smell.

The reverse mistake also happens. Two kitchens use different names for the
same herb, so visitors think they are different plants. One word may hide
several things. Several words may point to one thing.

When a fight hangs on a word, put the label down. Ask what was done, to whom,
under what conditions, and with what result. Then ask where the edge of the
word lies and who benefits from placing it there.

A good name helps you handle the thing. It does not replace looking at it.
